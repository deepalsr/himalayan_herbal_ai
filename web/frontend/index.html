Project Overview

An ML pipeline for antimicrobial drug discovery from Himalayan medicinal plant compounds. Predicts bioactivity and toxicity from molecular structure using descriptor-based neural networks.

Developed as a final-year BE Computer Engineering project at Cosmos College of Management and Technology, Pokhara University.

Dataset

309 unique compounds from 12 Himalayan medicinal plant species, supplemented with ChEMBL antibacterial assay data (S. aureus, IC50-labelled). Deduplicated by canonical SMILES.

Azadirachta indica Curcuma longa Berberis aristata Ocimum sanctum Withania somnifera Terminalia chebula Zingiber officinale Swertia chirayita ChEMBL:S.aureus

Pipeline

Key Finding

SHAP analysis reveals that molecular weight and hydrogen bond donors are the primary drivers of predicted antimicrobial activity, consistent with the structural complexity of natural product antibiotics. Notably, Lipinski violations contributed least — suggesting classical drug-likeness rules are poor proxies for antimicrobial activity, a finding consistent with known antibiotics that frequently violate the Rule of Five.

Scaffold-based evaluation reveals all models drop in ROC-AUC when tested on unseen scaffolds (~0.45–0.60 vs ~0.85 in random splits), motivating future graph-based representations (GAT) that may generalise beyond scaffold-level patterns.
